How New York Calculates Child Support

New York uses the Child Support Standards Act (CSSA) income shares model, applying statutory percentages to combined parental income after FICA deductions.

The New York CSSA Formula

BCSO = Combined AGI x CSSA %
BCSO

Basic Child Support Obligation — the total support amount before pro-rating between parents

Combined AGI

Combined adjusted gross income of both parents after FICA deductions (Social Security + Medicare)

CSSA %

Statutory percentage based on number of children (see table below)

NCP Obligation

Non-custodial parent's pro-rata share of the BCSO based on their proportion of combined income

Number of Children CSSA Percentage
1 child17%
2 children25%
3 children29%
4 children31%
5+ children35%

Key Factors in New York Calculations

New York's CSSA formula considers multiple factors when determining child support obligations

FICA Deductions

Gross income is adjusted by Social Security tax (6.2% up to the $176,100 wage base) and Medicare tax (1.45%) to arrive at each parent's adjusted gross income for child support purposes.

Income Cap

The CSSA percentage is applied to combined parental income up to $183,000 per year ($15,250/month). Courts have discretion for income above the cap based on the child's needs and lifestyle.

Low-Income Protections

If combined income falls below the poverty level ($15,650/year), a $25/month minimum applies. The self-support reserve ($21,128/year) ensures low-income parents retain enough for basic needs.

Mandatory Add-Ons

Childcare expenses, health insurance premiums for the children, and unreimbursed medical expenses exceeding $250 per child are added to the BCSO and split proportionally.

Pro-Rata Income Shares

Each parent's share of the total obligation is calculated based on their proportion of combined adjusted gross income, ensuring the obligation is equitably divided.

Self-Support Reserve

When the non-custodial parent's income is between the poverty level and the self-support reserve, the obligation is limited to the difference between their income and the poverty level, with a $50/month minimum.
