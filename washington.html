January 2026 WSCSS Updates Applied

Washington's child support schedule was significantly updated effective January 1, 2026 per Engrossed House Bill 1014. The economic table now extends to $50,000/month combined net income, the self-support reserve increased to 180% of the federal poverty guideline ($2,394/month), and new mandatory deductions include Paid Family & Medical Leave (PFML) and WA Cares. How Washington Calculates Child Support

Washington uses the Income Shares Model, combining both parents' net monthly incomes to look up the basic child support obligation from the official economic table.

The WSCSS Formula

BCSO = Economic Table Lookup(Combined Net Income, # Children)
Net Monthly Income

Gross income minus mandatory deductions: federal/state taxes, FICA, PFML, WA Cares, mandatory union dues, mandatory pension, and court-ordered maintenance paid

Combined Net Income

Sum of both parents' net monthly incomes (line 4 of the WSCSS worksheets), used to look up the BCSO from the economic table

BCSO

Basic Child Support Obligation from the 2026 economic table, based on combined net income and number of children (1-12)

Each Parent's Share

Each parent's proportional share of combined net income determines their portion of the BCSO (line 6 x line 5)

Key Factors in Washington Calculations

Washington's WSCSS considers multiple factors and includes important low-income protections

Self-Support Reserve (SSR)

Set at 180% of the federal poverty guideline ($2,394/month in 2026). When a parent's net income falls below the SSR, their obligation is limited to the presumptive $50 per child minimum.

Low-Income Combined Net

When combined net income is below $2,200/month, each parent's obligation defaults to the presumptive $50 per child minimum to protect both households' basic needs.

SSR Limitation (Line 8c)

When a parent's net income exceeds the SSR but the available amount (net minus SSR) is less than their basic CSO, the obligation is capped at the available amount or $50 per child, whichever is greater.

Economic Table (up to $50,000)

The 2026 economic table extends to $50,000/month combined net income (up from $12,000), covering the vast majority of cases without requiring extrapolation.

Healthcare & Childcare Add-Ons

Health insurance premiums and work-related childcare costs are added to the BCSO and split proportionally between parents based on their income shares (Part III of the worksheets).

Deviation Factors (RCW 26.19.075)

Courts may deviate from the standard calculation based on factors including preexisting child support obligations, shared residential schedules, and children from other relationships.
