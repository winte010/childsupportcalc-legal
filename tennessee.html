Current Tennessee Guidelines Applied

Tennessee's child support calculator uses the official DHS Income Shares schedule covering combined incomes from $150 to $28,250/month, with automatic high-income extrapolation above the schedule maximum. FICA rates updated for 2026 (SS 6.2% on $184,500 wage base + Medicare 1.45%).

How Tennessee Calculates Child Support

Tennessee uses the Income Shares Model, looking up the Basic Child Support Obligation from an official schedule based on combined parental income and number of children.

The Tennessee Income Shares Formula

BCSO = Schedule(Combined AGI, # Children)
BCSO

Basic Child Support Obligation from the official DHS schedule, based on combined income and number of children

Combined AGI

Combined adjusted gross income of both parents after FICA deductions and preexisting support obligations

Pro-Rata Share

Each parent's share of the BCSO based on their proportion of combined income

Parenting Time Credit

Adjustments for the alternative residential parent based on parenting days per year (92+ days = credit, 68 or fewer = increase)

Key Factors in Tennessee Calculations

Tennessee's Income Shares Model considers multiple factors to determine fair child support obligations

FICA Deductions

Gross income is adjusted by Social Security tax (6.2% up to the wage base cap) and Medicare tax (1.45%) to arrive at each parent's adjusted gross income. Self-employment FICA is also supported.

Official DHS Schedule

The BCSO is determined from the Tennessee Department of Human Services income schedule, covering combined monthly incomes from $150 to $28,250 in $50 increments for 1 through 5 children.

Parenting Time Adjustments

Tennessee adjusts obligations based on actual parenting days. Parents with 92+ days receive a credit reducing their obligation. Parents with 68 or fewer days see an increase. Standard presumption is 80 days.

High-Income Extrapolation

For combined incomes above $28,250/month, Tennessee uses statutory extrapolation percentages (6.81% to 8.66% depending on children) to extend the schedule transparently.

Self-Support Reserve

Tennessee protects $957/month of each parent's income (90% of the federal poverty level), ensuring parents retain enough for basic needs. The 85% rule limits obligation to 85% of income above the reserve.

Add-On Expenses

Work-related childcare costs and children's health insurance premiums are added to the BCSO and divided proportionally between parents based on their income shares.
